Dynamic Model's Profile Reconstruction (Lines 354-369)

Code adds Ft_V = 0.0 at the feed stage

Why the Discrepancy Persists
Energy Balance is Trying to Correct
Looking at vflow_energy_calc  at t=3.0s:
Stage 11: wants 13,293 lbmol/h (using 11,954)
Stage 12: wants 22,840 lbmol/h (using 14,169)
Stage 13: wants 12,891 lbmol/h (using 11,938)
The energy balance recognizes something is wrong and wants to increase vapor flows even more, especially at
stage 12. This suggests the thermal state is confused - the model thinks it needs MORE vapor to balance the
energy, when actually it needs LESS.
Why Energy Balance Can't Fix It
The energy balance vapor flow calculation (lines 627+) computes:
This assumes:
Liquid enthalpy is known and correct
Vapor enthalpy is known and correct
The target dT/dt  is appropriate
But if the composition is wrong (because mass balances used the wrong vapor flow), then:
Enthalpies are wrong
K-values are wrong
Everything drifts further from steady state
Relaxation is Damping the Correction
The model has vapor_flow_relaxation_sec  enabled, which blends:
This prevents wild oscillations, but also prevents the model from correcting the fundamental profile error.
The Correct Fix: Account for Feed Thermal State
V_out[i] = (energy_in - energy_out + Q - dE_target) / (HV_out - HL_out)
V_used = alpha * V_energy + (1 - alpha) * V_prev

--- PAGE 6 ---
Rectifying section (stages 2-11): V increases from 7,406 → 8,333 lbmol/h (~13% variation)
Stripping section (stages 13-20): V increases from 11,123 → 11,743 lbmol/h (~5% variation)
This violates constant molar overflow because:
Different components have different latent heats
Propane (more volatile): 7,960 BTU/lbmol
Butane (medium): 9,320 BTU/lbmol
Pentane (heavier): 10,500 BTU/lbmol
As composition changes up the column:
More propane at top → less heat per mole vaporized → need more moles
More pentane at bottom → more heat per mole vaporized → need fewer moles
The energy-based vapor flow model (vapor_flow_model="energy" ) SHOULD capture this, but it's starting from
the wrong initial condition.
Impact on Simulation
Why the Model Seems to "Work"
The simulation doesn't immediately blow up because:
1. Energy balance sees the mismatch and tries to adjust V_out
2. Relaxation prevents wild swings
3. Below feed (~4% error) is close enough to maintain stability
Why It Will Eventually Drift
Over time:
1. Composition drift: Wrong vapor flows → wrong component splits → compositions drift from steady
state
2. Temperature drift: Energy balance can't maintain correct temperatures with wrong flows
3. Pressure errors: Hydraulic model uses vapor flows to calculate pressure drops
4. Accumulation/depletion: Mass balance errors accumulate as holdup changes

Additional Notes
Why This Wasn't Caught Earlier
The comments at lines 211-219 actually EXPLAIN this issue:
"If an explicit Feed stream is present, the steady-state internal L/V profiles loaded from Excel/ChemSep
typically already include the feed effect (step changes at the feed stage). If we ALSO add feed as an explicit
source term, we double-count it..."
But then the code DOES rebuild the profiles anyway when feed_stage0 is not None ! This is contradictory.
The reconstruction was probably added to handle cases where:
No internal profiles provided
Profiles don't sum correctly with boundary conditions
Simplified constant-overflow model desired
But it breaks for real columns with feed flashing.
Why "vapor_fraction=0" in Feed Spec
ChemSep shows the feed as "7142.98 L", meaning it enters as saturated/subcooled liquid. The vapor_fraction
field in your feed spec correctly reflects this as 0.0 or near-zero.
The flashing occurs ON the tray, not in the feed pipe. This is a tray phenomenon, not a feed specification issue.
